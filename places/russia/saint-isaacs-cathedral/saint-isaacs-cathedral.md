## Overview

Saint Isaac's Cathedral is one of the largest domed churches in the world. Its gilded dome rises about 101.5 metres over the centre of Saint Petersburg, and its four porticos are held up by more than 100 enormous red granite columns, each carved from a single block of stone. It took 40 years to build.

## The main story

- **Before 1818** – Earlier churches dedicated to Saint Isaac stand on or near the site.
- **1818** – Construction begins to the design of the French architect Auguste de Montferrand. The building stands on marshy ground, on a foundation of thousands of wooden piles.
- **1818–1858** – The giant granite columns, quarried in Finland, are brought by sea and raised into place. The dome is gilded using a mixture of gold and mercury; the toxic vapours are reported to have killed many workers (one figure often given is 60).
- **1858** – The cathedral is consecrated. Montferrand dies shortly afterwards.
- **20th century** – Turned into a museum in the Soviet era. Services have since resumed.

## The alternative view

### Giant single-block columns

The cathedral's 112 granite columns are each a single block, with weights given anywhere from about 64 to 114 tonnes. The official story is that they were quarried, shipped and raised with 19th-century methods, using manpower and capstans. For old world researchers, monolithic columns like these are among the strongest signs of a more advanced lost building technology.

### Forty years of construction

Building took 40 years, even with the resources of the Russian Empire. What exactly took so long, and are the records of each stage complete?

### Built on older churches

Earlier churches stood on or near the site. How much of the current building's foundations or structure was inherited from them?

### Mercury and gold

The dome was gilded using mercury, a method that killed workers. Gilded domes appear on buildings across the old world. The alternative view sometimes links domes and their metals to ideas about lost energy technology.

### What would support the alternative view

- Evidence that the columns or other parts predate 1818
- Gaps in the records of quarrying, transport and raising of the columns
- Structures under the cathedral not explained by the earlier churches

### What would weaken it

- Montferrand's own published account of the construction
- Engravings and records showing the columns being quarried and raised
- Records of the earlier churches and the foundations

## Open questions for contributors

- **Columns:** How many columns are there (112 is most often given), and what do they weigh?
- **Mercury deaths:** How many workers died from mercury vapour?
- **Raising the columns:** How exactly were they lifted, and are there contemporary drawings?
- **Foundations:** How many wooden piles were used, and what is underneath?
- **Photos:** Images of the construction from 1818–1858 would be especially useful.
