## Overview

The Palace of Westminster is the home of the UK Parliament, on the north bank of the Thames. Almost all of what stands today was built after a great fire in 1834 destroyed the old palace. Its clock tower, officially the Elizabeth Tower and widely known as Big Ben after its great bell, is one of the most famous clock towers in the world.

## The main story

- **11th century onwards** – A royal palace stands on the site. Westminster Hall dates from 1097.
- **16 October 1834** – A fire destroys most of the old palace. It is started by the burning of old wooden tally sticks, the Exchequer's former accounting records, in furnaces under the House of Lords. Westminster Hall, the Jewel Tower and parts of St Stephen's Chapel survive.
- **1836** – Charles Barry's Gothic design is chosen from 97 entries. Augustus Pugin designs much of the detail and the clock tower.
- **1840 onwards** – Construction begins. Work continues for around 30 years.
- **1859** – The great clock starts and the bell "Big Ben" first strikes.
- **2012** – The clock tower is renamed the Elizabeth Tower for Queen Elizabeth II's Diamond Jubilee.

## The alternative view

### Records burned

The fire that destroyed the old palace was started by deliberately burning centuries of old government accounting records (tally sticks). The alternative view asks: what other records were lost, and was the destruction of the old palace and its contents really an accident?

### A "medieval" palace built in the 1800s

The new palace was designed to look medieval, with Gothic towers, spires and carved stone. Gothic Revival buildings like this appeared across the world in the same decades. How easy is it today to tell which parts of "old" Gothic buildings are really medieval and which are 19th-century?

### Built over the old

The new palace was built around the surviving medieval Westminster Hall and on the old foundations. What earlier structures lie under it?

### The clock tower

Clock towers appear in very similar forms across the world in this period. What was the tower designed for beyond holding a clock, and what lies in its foundations?

### What would support the alternative view

- Structures under the palace older than the known medieval buildings
- Evidence about what was destroyed in 1834 beyond the tally sticks
- Gaps in the construction records for the new palace

### What would weaken it

- The official inquiry into the 1834 fire
- Barry's and Pugin's drawings, contracts and accounts
- Photographs and engravings of the construction

## Open questions for contributors

- **Completion:** When was the new palace actually finished: 1860, 1867 or 1870?
- **Clock tower:** Was the tower complete in 1857 or 1859?
- **The fire inquiry:** What did the 1835 inquiry conclude?
- **Underground:** What lies beneath the palace and the river frontage?
- **Photos:** Photos and engravings from 1834–1870 would be especially useful.
