## Overview

Dresden Hauptbahnhof is the city's main station, south of the old town. It's unusual because it combines two kinds of station: terminus tracks end in the central hall, while through tracks run past on raised platforms on either side. Its central entrance hall is topped by a dome. Since 2006 the platforms have been covered by a translucent fabric roof that lets in daylight.

## The main story

- **1892** – Giese and Weidner of Dresden, with Arwed Roßbach of Leipzig, win the design competition.
- **1892–1898** – The station is built on the site of an earlier station. It opens in 1898 (Wikipedia gives 23 April 1898; some sources say the work ran 1892–1897).
- **February 1945** – The station is badly damaged in the Allied bombing of Dresden. It is crowded with refugees at the time, and many people are killed there. It is damaged but not completely destroyed.
- **Post-war** – Repaired. The original partly glazed roofs are covered over with timber, making the halls dark.
- **1997–2006** – Restored by Foster + Partners, who replace the heavy roof with a lightweight, translucent PTFE-coated glass-fibre membrane.

## The alternative view

### Two stations in one

The station mixes a terminus and a through station on two levels, with tracks raised above the street. In old world research, raised levels and platforms are sometimes linked to mud flood theories. Was the station designed this way from the start, or was the ground level different when it was built?

### Built in six years on an older site

The new station replaced an earlier one on the same site. How much of the old station's foundations or structure was reused, and was the six-year build really from scratch?

### The dome

The domed central hall echoes domes on stations, exhibition halls and palaces across Europe at the same time. The alternative view asks whether this shared style points to a common, older origin.

### Destruction and change

After the 1945 bombing the station was repaired, but its glass roofs were covered with timber for decades. What was lost, and what original features were hidden or removed during post-war repairs?

### What would support the alternative view

- Evidence that the street or ground level around the station has changed since the 1890s
- Older structures beneath or within the 1890s building
- Gaps in the construction records

### What would weaken it

- The competition designs and building records of Giese, Weidner and Roßbach
- Dated photographs of the construction and of the earlier station
- Survey findings from the 1997–2006 restoration

## Open questions for contributors

- **Opening date:** 1897 or 1898?
- **The earlier station:** What stood on the site before, and what was kept?
- **Roof size:** Sources give the new roof as 22,000 m² or 30,000 m². Which is the membrane itself?
- **The dome:** Is there a statue or sculpture on top of the central hall, and what does it represent?
- **Photos:** Historic photos and postcards from before 1900 are likely to be public domain.
