## Overview

Cologne Cathedral is a vast Gothic cathedral beside the Rhine, with two spires about 157 metres tall. Its construction is one of the strangest stories in architecture: building started in 1248, stopped for nearly 400 years with a medieval crane left standing on the unfinished south tower, and then resumed in 1842. It was finally finished in 1880, following medieval plans.

## The main story

- **1248** – The foundation stone is laid. The cathedral is built to house the relics of the Three Kings.
- **1473** – Work stops, with the choir finished and the south tower built only to the belfry level. A wooden crane stays on top of the tower for around 400 years.
- **Early 1800s** – Interest in finishing the cathedral grows. Medieval drawings of the west front are rediscovered.
- **1842** – Work resumes, following the medieval plans but using modern methods, including an iron roof structure.
- **1880** – The cathedral is completed, 632 years after it was begun. At about 157 m, it is often said to have briefly been the tallest building in the world.
- **1939–1945** – Hit many times in Allied bombing but remains standing.

## The alternative view

### 632 years, or something else?

The official story is a medieval building left unfinished for four centuries, then completed in 38 years in the 19th century to the original design. The alternative view asks whether the medieval and 19th-century "halves" are really as distinct as the story says, or whether the dating of the different parts is less certain than claimed.

### Plans rediscovered at the right time

The medieval drawings that guided the completion are said to have been rediscovered just as the movement to finish the cathedral began. How were they found, and how do we know they're medieval?

### Iron hidden in a Gothic shell

The 19th-century builders used an iron roof structure inside a Gothic exterior, using new technology while matching medieval work. Where else might modern materials be hidden behind "old" stone?

### Standing amid ruins

In 1945 the cathedral stood almost alone among the ruins of central Cologne. Why did it survive when so much around it did not?

### What would support the alternative view

- Construction evidence that doesn't match the official medieval and 19th-century phases
- Doubts about the age or origin of the rediscovered plans
- Structures beneath the cathedral that predate 1248, beyond the known earlier churches

### What would weaken it

- The cathedral works' (Dombauhütte) continuous records
- Scientific dating of the stonework and timber
- Archaeological excavations under the cathedral
