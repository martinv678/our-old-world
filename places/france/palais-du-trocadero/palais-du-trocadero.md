## Overview

The Palais du Trocadéro stood on Chaillot hill, facing the Seine and the Champ de Mars. It had a huge circular concert hall flanked by two tall towers, and two long curved wings. Built for the 1878 world's fair, it was never popular, and for the 1937 exhibition it was largely torn down and rebuilt as today's Palais de Chaillot.

## The main story

- **1876–1878** – Built for the Exposition Universelle of 1878 to designs by Gabriel Davioud and Jules Bourdais, with a central rotunda for concerts and two towers.
- **1878 onwards** – Used as a concert hall and museum, including an ethnographic museum and a museum of French monuments.
- **1935–1937** – The central rotunda and towers are demolished for the 1937 International Exhibition. The curved wings are kept, encased and extended to form the new Palais de Chaillot. Sources differ on whether demolition is dated 1935 or 1937.

## The alternative view

### Gone in under 60 years

A palace with a vast domed rotunda and two towers lasted less than 60 years before being torn down, at great expense, for a new building in a completely different style. The alternative view asks why so many grand 19th-century buildings were removed so quickly.

### The new building wraps the old

The Palais de Chaillot reused the Trocadéro's wings and foundations, keeping its basic shape. How much of the 1878 structure is still hidden inside? And how much of the 1878 building itself reused something older on the hill?

### Moorish towers in Paris

The towers and rotunda were described as Moorish or "oriental", like many buildings in this repo: Sammezzano, Villa Crespi and the Royal Pavilion. The alternative view sees this shared style as possibly older than the official dates suggest.

### Part of a world's fair pattern

Like the Crystal Palace, Chicago's White City and the Melbourne exhibition annexes, the Trocadéro was a world's fair building that didn't survive. Old world research often asks whether world's fairs presented existing buildings as new, then removed them.

### What would support the alternative view

- Structures under or inside the Palais de Chaillot older than 1876
- Gaps in the construction records for the 1878 building
- Evidence about what was on Chaillot hill before 1876

### What would weaken it

- Davioud's and Bourdais's drawings and contracts
- Photographs of the construction in 1876–78 and of the demolition in 1935–37
- Records of what was kept and reused in the Palais de Chaillot

## Open questions for contributors

- **Demolition dates:** When exactly were the rotunda and towers taken down?
- **What survives:** Which parts of the 1878 building are inside the Palais de Chaillot today?
- **The hill:** What was on the site before 1876, and what is underneath it?
- **The towers:** How tall were they, and what were they used for?
- **Photos:** Photos and engravings from 1878–1900 are likely to be public domain.
