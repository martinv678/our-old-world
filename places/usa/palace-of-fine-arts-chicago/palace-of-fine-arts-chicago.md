## Overview

The Palace of Fine Arts is the only major building of Chicago's 1893 World's Columbian Exposition, the "White City", still standing. Its classical front, domes and columns now house the Museum of Science and Industry, one of the largest science museums in the world.

## The main story

- **1890–1893** – Chicago builds the World's Columbian Exposition in Jackson Park: a "White City" of huge classical palaces. Most are built as temporary structures covered in *staff*, a plaster and fibre mix. The Palace of Fine Arts, designed by Charles B. Atwood, is built with a brick structure under its plaster because it has to protect valuable artworks.
- **1893** – The fair runs from May to October.
- **1894** – Fires destroy most of the remaining fair buildings. The Palace of Fine Arts survives and becomes the Columbian Museum, later the Field Museum of Natural History.
- **1920** – The Field Museum moves out and the building is left empty and decaying.
- **c. 1929–1933** – Funded by Julius Rosenwald, the exterior is rebuilt in limestone as an exact copy of the original, and the interior is remodelled in Art Moderne style. Sources date the rebuild 1928–32 or 1929–33.
- **1933–1940** – Opens in stages as the Museum of Science and Industry.

## The alternative view

### A "temporary" city

The official story is that a whole city of vast, ornate classical palaces was built in about two years as temporary buildings, then mostly lost to fire within months of closing. For old world research the White City is one of the most important cases: was it really built from scratch for a six-month fair, or was something older presented and then removed?

### Fire again

As with the Crystal Palace, fire played a big part in erasing the fair. The 1894 fires destroyed most of the remaining buildings. Was it bad luck, or a convenient way to clear the site?

### The one that survived, then was replaced

The single surviving palace was then rebuilt in stone, copying its old exterior while gutting the inside. What original fabric, if any, survives inside the 1930s stone shell?

### What would support the alternative view

- Foundations or structures under Jackson Park older than 1890
- Gaps in the construction records for the fair buildings
- Original 1893 fabric inside the museum that doesn't match the official story

### What would weaken it

- The fair's detailed construction records, contracts and photographs
- The many dated photographs of the White City being built
- Records of the 1929–33 rebuild

## Open questions for contributors

- **Rebuild dates:** 1928–32 or 1929–33?
- **What survives:** How much of Atwood's 1893 brick structure is still inside?
- **The fires:** What caused the 1894 fires, and what reports were made?
- **Underground:** What is beneath the museum and the old fairgrounds?
- **Photos:** Photos of the 1893 fair are public domain and widely available.
