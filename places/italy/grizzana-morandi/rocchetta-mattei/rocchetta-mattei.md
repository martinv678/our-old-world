# Rocchetta Mattei

| | |
|---|---|
| **Location** | Località Savignano, Riola, Grizzana Morandi (BO), Emilia-Romagna, Italy |
| **Near** | Bologna (approx. 40 km south-west) |
| **Built** | From 1850, on the ruins of a medieval fortress; extended until the 1890s |
| **Architect** | Count Cesare Mattei (owner and designer) |
| **Style** | Eclectic: Moorish, Gothic, Renaissance |
| **Original use** | Private residence and base for Mattei's medical practice |
| **Status** | Restored by Fondazione Carisbo; open for guided visits |

## Overview

Rocchetta Mattei is a fairy-tale castle on a rocky outcrop above the Reno valley in the Bolognese Apennines. Its towers, onion domes and courtyards mix Moorish, medieval and Renaissance styles. Its best-known features include a courtyard modelled on the Court of the Lions at the Alhambra and a Moorish-style chapel where Mattei is buried.

## The main story

- **1809** – Cesare Mattei is born in Bologna into a wealthy family.
- **1850** – Mattei buys the site, with the ruins of a 13th-century fortress, and begins building. Some sources say he moved in around 1856.
- **1850s–1890s** – Mattei develops **electrohomeopathy**, a system of plant-based remedies he said worked through "vegetable electricity". Patients come from across Europe and his remedies are exported abroad. He keeps building and changing the castle throughout this time.
- **1896** – Mattei dies and is buried in the castle chapel.
- **20th century** – The castle passes to his adopted son, Mario Venturoli, and later to a local businessman who runs it as a hotel and restaurant. It is abandoned in the 1980s.
- **2005** – Bought by Fondazione Carisbo, which begins stabilisation and restoration.
- **2015** – Reopened for guided visits. Some sources give an earlier date.

Mattei was famous enough in his lifetime to be mentioned in Dostoevsky's *The Brothers Karamazov*.

## The alternative view

### Electricity, medicine and the "old world"

At the centre of the story is a count who claimed to heal people with "electricity" stored in liquids, and who built a castle of domes and towers to work from. In alternative history circles, domes, spires and towers are sometimes linked to theories of lost "atmospheric" or "free" energy. Was electrohomeopathy just a 19th-century health fad, or a half-remembered fragment of older knowledge about energy and healing?

### Built on something older

The official story already admits the castle stands on medieval ruins. How much of the structure is really 1850s work, and how much was older and simply refaced? The line between "ruin" and "new build" is worth checking.

### Moorish design in the Apennines

As with Sammezzano and Villa Crespi, a Moorish-style building appears in a part of Italy with no Moorish tradition. The alternative view asks whether these Orientalist buildings across Europe share an older common origin.

### A building that was never finished

Mattei kept building for over 40 years, and his heirs kept changing it. A building that is always being "added to" makes it hard to say what is original and when each part was made.

### Mattei's secret formulas

Mattei never fully revealed how his remedies were made, and the formulas were passed on privately after his death. Missing or hidden knowledge is a recurring theme in alternative history.

### What would support the alternative view

- Structure or decoration clearly older than 1850 beyond the known medieval ruins
- Construction gaps in the records, or no accounts for major parts of the build
- Evidence that Mattei's ideas drew on older sources he did not name

### What would weaken it

- Mattei's building accounts, drawings or correspondence
- Survey reports from the Fondazione Carisbo restoration dating each phase
- Clear documentation of how electrohomeopathic remedies were made
