## Overview

Palmanova is a whole town built as a star fort. Seen from above, it is a perfect nine-pointed star: three rings of walls and moats around a town laid out on a radial plan, with streets leading to a central hexagonal square. It was founded by the Republic of Venice in 1593 and is still lived in today.

## The main story

- **7 October 1593** – Founded by Venice as a fortress to defend its eastern border against the Habsburgs and Ottoman raids.
- **1593 onwards** – The first two rings of fortifications are built, with three monumental gates (Porta Udine, Porta Cividale and Porta Aquileia).
- **Early 1800s** – Under Napoleonic rule, a third, outer ring of fortifications is added.
- **1960** – Declared a national monument.
- **9 July 2017** – Inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List as part of the *Venetian Works of Defence between the 16th and 17th Centuries*.

## The alternative view

### Star forts everywhere

Palmanova is one of the most perfect examples of a star fort. Similar star-shaped forts appear across Europe, the Americas, Africa and Asia, with very similar geometry. The alternative view asks whether star forts were really only military, or whether their shape served another purpose (some theories suggest energy, water or sound) that was later forgotten.

### Geometry seen best from the air

The star shape and the town's radial plan are only fully visible from above. Why design something so precise that its builders could never see it whole?

### A town that struggled to fill

Palmanova was planned as a working town, but it is often said to have struggled to attract residents in its early years. Was the town built for the population, or were people brought in to occupy a structure that already existed?

### What would support the alternative view

- Structures or foundations older than 1593 within the walls
- Records showing the fortifications were never seriously used for defence
- Features of the layout that serve no clear military purpose

### What would weaken it

- Venetian building records, contracts and plans from 1593 onwards
- Records of the Napoleonic works on the outer ring
- Archaeological surveys of the walls and moats
