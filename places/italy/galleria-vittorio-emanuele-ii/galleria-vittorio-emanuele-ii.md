## Overview

The Galleria Vittorio Emanuele II is one of the oldest and grandest shopping arcades in the world. Two glass-roofed streets cross in the shape of a cross, meeting under a great glass and iron dome. It links Milan's cathedral square with La Scala, and its mosaic floors and painted façades make it one of the city's best-known landmarks.

## The main story

- **1861–1863** – After Italian unification, Milan holds a competition to redesign the area north of the cathedral. Giuseppe Mengoni wins.
- **7 March 1865** – The foundation stone is laid.
- **1867** – The main arcade is inaugurated in the presence of King Vittorio Emanuele II.
- **30 December 1877** – Mengoni dies after falling from scaffolding, the day before the triumphal arch facing the cathedral square is due to be inaugurated. Accounts differ on where he fell, and some have suggested it wasn't an accident.
- **Second World War** – Damaged by bombing and later restored.

## The alternative view

### Built in two years

The main arcade, with its iron and glass roof and dome, is said to have opened about two years after the foundation stone was laid. Like other iron and glass buildings of the period, the speed raises questions about whether existing structures or older technology were involved.

### The architect's death

Mengoni died in a fall the day before the final inauguration, and the circumstances have never been fully explained. Unexplained deaths around major buildings come up in alternative history research. What did Mengoni know, and why do the accounts of his fall differ?

### The same arcades worldwide

Glass-roofed arcades with a central dome appear in many cities in the same decades, from Naples to Moscow and beyond. The alternative view asks whether this reflects a shared older design tradition.

### What would support the alternative view

- Structures under the arcade older than 1865
- Gaps or contradictions in the construction records
- Evidence about Mengoni's death that contradicts the official account

### What would weaken it

- Mengoni's competition drawings and the construction contracts
- Photographs of the construction from 1865–1877
- Official reports into Mengoni's death
