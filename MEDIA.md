# Media

Every image on the site in one place: photographs, postcards, engravings and maps. Click an image on the website to enlarge it.

The page has two parts:

- **Found images.** Interesting images the owner has come across that don't (yet) belong to a building entry: book illustrations, screenshots, odd photographs. These are added by hand.
- **Building images.** Every image saved in a building's `images/` folder, grouped by country and building, with the caption it has in the entry. This part is generated automatically by `scripts/build_index.py`, so there's no need to edit it by hand.

## Found images

To add one, save it in `media/images/` (short lowercase file name with hyphens, under about 1600px wide), then add a block below with the image, a caption (date, author, source and licence, as far as they're known) and any notes. Label claims with the evidence labels (Documented, Observed, Reported, Speculative) where it helps.

### The Tabernacle ("Das Heiligthum"), from a German Bible

<div class="media-grid" markdown>

![Engraving of the Tabernacle drawn as a temple of slender columns with Corinthian capitals and a decorated roof, with curtains being drawn over a long frame by men on ladders, and robed figures in the foreground, on a page of German text printed in Fraktur](media/images/tabernacle-engraving-german-bible.jpg)
*Engraving captioned "Das Heiligthum o[der] …" ("The sanctuary, or …"), from a German Bible printed in Fraktur type, photographed with a finger pointing at the page. Supplied by the owner; the photograph appears to be a still from a video. Edition, date, photographer and licence to be confirmed.*

</div>

- **What it shows.** The Tabernacle, the portable sanctuary of the Israelites described in Exodus 25–27 and 36–38. It is drawn cut away: on the left a columned structure with Corinthian capitals and a ceiling decorated with cherubim and flowers, on the right a long frame being covered with curtains by men on ladders. Two parts are lettered **A** and **B**, so the original page probably had a key.
- **The surrounding text.** The page is from an alphabetical word list (a Bible dictionary or glossary): the entries visible are *Talent* ("Geldsumme; sein Werth, je nachdem es Gold oder Silber [war], betrug ungefähr 1500, … 23,000 Thaler", a sum of money worth about 1,500 or 23,000 thalers depending on whether gold or silver) and *Tammuz* ("Götzenbild Adonis", the idol Adonis). **Observed.**
- **Dating clue.** The spellings *Heiligthum* and *Werth*, and prices given in thalers, point to a book printed before the German spelling reform of 1901–1902 and probably before the mark replaced the thaler in 1873–1876. **Observed**; this is an inference from the spelling, not a confirmed date.
- **Why it's interesting.** Exodus describes the Tabernacle as boards of acacia wood overlaid with gold, standing in silver sockets, with curtains and coverings of skins. The engraver has instead drawn classical columns with Corinthian capitals, a style from Greece many centuries later. That could simply be the 19th-century habit of drawing ancient buildings in the architecture of the day, or a sign that the artist was working from older temple images. **Speculative.**
- **Leads.** Identify the edition: search the Internet Archive, Google Books and the Münchener DigitalisierungsZentrum for German Bibles with a word list containing "Das Heiligthum oder die Stiftshütte" (the usual German name for the Tabernacle). Find the video the still was taken from, which should name the book.
